EQUATE: Towards Globally Equitable Language Technologies

2024 — Present · With Anna Korhonen (PI) and the Language Technology Lab, University of Cambridge

EQUATE works to make language AI more equitable across the world's 7,000+ languages. Most language technology serves a small set of dominant, Global-North languages, leaving the majority of the world's linguistic communities digitally underrepresented — EQUATE builds tools and partnerships to change that.

Motivation

AI's benefits are unevenly distributed by language. Communities without "high-resource" languages are locked out of tools the rest of the world takes for granted, which compounds existing gaps in digital infrastructure and representation.

Approach

The project has two main strands: the EQUATE Language AI Readiness Index, which assesses how ready a language and its community are for technology adoption by combining AI-resource, digital-infrastructure, and socioeconomic indicators; and COACT, a community-centred, participatory, actionable roadmap to equitable language AI, drawing on NLP, HCI, international development, and participatory research.

Case studies

Case studies examine real development challenges: disaster risk reduction and cultural preservation in Nepal, green jobs and climate resilience in Uganda, cultural preservation in Peru, and technology-needs assessments in Indonesia — alongside technical research into small language models and culturally-aware, transparent NLP approaches.

Equity in language AI isn't just about better translation — it's about who gets to participate in building the tools that shape their own digital future.

Outcomes & impact

EQUATE is ongoing, led by Anna Korhonen and the Language Technology Lab at the University of Cambridge, with funding from the European Research Council and UK Research and Innovation.
